The nonprofit home for volunteer-run communities.

Community Builder Toolbox (CBT) gives volunteer communities a legal home, signed contracts, and a working playbook. Since 2018, CBT has carried ZaiGeZaiGu's Spring Festival Galas, singing contests and culture fairs. Today CBT is the nonprofit home of six volunteer communities in the Bay Area and Seattle.

The toolbox

What we learned running volunteer events.

Every volunteer community runs into the same four problems: money, time, people and handover. Here is what works for us.

01Set the budget before you plan the event+

If sponsorship is uncertain, every meeting turns into a budget review. Decide the money first, then size the event to fit.

  • Allocate a fixed budget per event and per line item from past years' numbers.
  • Keep a small reserve. This year's surplus or deficit funds next year, not this one.
  • Protect the small things that keep people coming back, such as thank-you gifts for judges and volunteers.
02Start early and work back from the show date+

A short preparation window is the most common reason a show feels rushed. Fix the venue first, then build the timeline backward.

  • Book large venues many months ahead. Venue contracts set your date.
  • Set milestones for sponsorship, auditions, rehearsals, promotion and ticketing.
  • Hold a kickoff meeting for all teams so that every team sees the same timeline.
03Recruit for every team, not only the stage+

Volunteers give time because of the community and the experience. When too few people join, team leads end up doing everything.

  • Recruit early and publish a clear list of roles: stage, sound, lighting, design, sponsorship, operations.
  • Write down the scope of each request between teams, for example what the design team must deliver, in what sizes and by when.
  • Pair new volunteers with returning ones.
04Hand over gradually, not all at once+

When the whole production team changes every year, each new team relearns the same lessons. Gradual handover keeps the knowledge.

  • Keep part of last year's leads as advisors or co-leads.
  • Train a second person for hard-to-fill roles such as lighting and sound.
  • Hold a short retrospective after each event and store it where the next team will find it.
05Put every agreement in writing+

Sponsors, venues and partners all deserve a signed agreement. Written agreements protect volunteers and make the next year easier.

  • Use a template for each type: cash sponsorship, in-kind sponsorship, media partnership, venue rental.
  • Sign electronically and file each agreement in one shared place.
  • Record what you promised each sponsor and check it off after the event.
